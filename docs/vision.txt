Perspicua Vision: The Kernel You Can See Through
================================================

This file is the NORTH STAR (the "why" and the "where"). order.txt sequences
the work, ideas.txt catalogs it; this file says what it is all FOR. When two
designs are otherwise tied, the one that serves this vision wins. When a new
feature is proposed, it is weighed against this file before it enters ideas.txt.

Adopted: August 2026. This is a direction, not a rewrite plan — read "WHAT THIS
IS NOT" before drawing conclusions.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE THESIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Perspicua" is Latin for clear, transparent, easy to see through. The project's
identity is that sentence, taken literally:

    Perspicua is an operating system whose defining property is that it can
    always explain itself — and, having explained a failure, recover from it
    without rebooting the machine.

Observability and recovery are the ORGANIZING PRINCIPLE of the kernel, not a
debugging feature bolted on afterwards. Most systems treat tracing, invariant
checking, and fault recovery as optional extras that cost performance and are
compiled out of production builds. Perspicua inverts that: the always-on flight
recorder, the invariant engine, and restartable subsystems ARE the
architecture, and every other subsystem is built to feed and to survive them.

Two concrete claims define the identity:

  CLAIM 1 — THE BLACK BOX. The kernel is a flight recorder in the aviation
    sense: every lock transition, IRQ, syscall, scheduling decision, and
    subsystem state change streams into an always-on, clock-stamped ring
    buffer that survives crashes and warm reboots. No failure is ever
    unexplained. "It hung" is not an acceptable bug report against this
    kernel, because the kernel itself makes it impossible.

  CLAIM 2 — RESTARTABLE CELLS. Subsystems are cells: units with their own
    memory arenas, an explicit ledger of every resource they hold, and
    message-mediated entry points. A fault attributed to a cell tears down
    and reincarnates THAT CELL — the SD driver, a mounted filesystem — not
    the machine. Panic is the last resort, not the first response.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS DIRECTION 
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The research question: MINIX 3 proved subsystem reincarnation for
microkernels, where address spaces isolate the pieces. Perspicua asks how much
of that resilience a MONOLITH can get — without paying for address-space
isolation, using ownership ledgers, arena allocation, and runtime invariants
instead. Even a partial answer is interesting: "cells recover N% of injected
faults" is a finding with numbers, from a fault-injection campaign we control.
The claim stays honest at every stage of construction.

The production niche: unattended Raspberry Pis — kiosks, sensor nodes, small
fleets — where "walk over and reflash it" is the failure mode that kills
projects. A kernel that diagnoses itself after a crash and restarts a wedged
driver instead of hanging is genuinely useful there, independent of the
research value.

Why us: the three ingredients that are hard to retrofit into a mature kernel —
a lock dependency tracker (core/lockdep.c), a live kernel debugger
(core/kdb.c), and a procfs reporting culture — already exist and are already
load-bearing. This direction promotes them from tools to architecture. No
other direction we considered (see "ROADS NOT TAKEN") reuses as much of what
is already built, or pays back every milestone as immediately.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS IS NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

NOT a departure from the Unix personality. The shell, the VFS, procfs, signals,
  job control, the POSIX-ish syscall layer — all of it stays and keeps
  growing. The vision changes what the kernel IS underneath the personality,
  not how it presents to userspace. A user at the shell sees a normal system;
  a user at kdb sees a system that can explain every decision it ever made.

NOT a microkernel rewrite. Cells are NOT processes and do not get address
  spaces. Drivers stay in the kernel, at EL1, called at function-call cost.
  Isolation is by discipline (arenas, ledgers, mediated entry points) checked
  by machinery (invariants, lockdep, the recorder) — that trade-off IS the
  research question, so we hold to it deliberately.

NOT observability as an optional build flag. The recorder runs always,
  including release builds, under a hard overhead budget (see MEASURES OF
  SUCCESS). If an event is too expensive to record, the fix is a cheaper
  record format, not recording less.

NOT a reason to skip the foundation. Phases 1-5 of order.txt stand: a flight
  recorder wired into a kernel with lost-wakeup bugs records lies. The vision
  reshapes WHAT WE BUILD NEXT and HOW we build it (see DESIGN RULES), it does
  not excuse skipping the hardening the audit demanded.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FOUR PILLARS  (V1-V4 — these tags are used throughout order.txt)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

V1. THE FLIGHT RECORDER
    One clock-stamped binary event stream for the whole kernel. Lockdep
    transitions, scheduler decisions, IRQ entry/exit, syscall entry/exit,
    signal delivery, cell lifecycle — all producers feed ONE ring with one
    timebase (the generic timer's counter), so cross-subsystem causality is
    readable directly from the record. Readout through kdb and /proc;
    binary records with a text formatter, never printf strings at the
    producer (too slow, and it loses structure).
    The recorder's backing store is physical RAM reserved outside the PMM,
    with a magic + CRC header. On the Pi 4 a watchdog reset does not clear
    RAM, so after a crash the NEXT boot still holds the pre-crash history:
    that is the black-box property, and it is what turns a hang into a
    readable story. Reset-surviving bits in the PM block carry a reboot
    reason code even through a hard watchdog reset.
    Seeds already planned: the dmesg ring (order.txt Phase 2 item 8), the
    watchdog (Phase 2 item 9), strace-lite (Phase 3 item 6) — each is
    specced to grow INTO the recorder rather than beside it.

V2. CELLS  (restartable subsystems)
    A cell is a subsystem unit — a driver instance, a mounted filesystem —
    with: (a) its own slab arenas, so its allocations are attributable and
    revocable in bulk; (b) a resource LEDGER enumerating every IRQ, page,
    lock, device, and timer it holds; (c) message-mediated entry points, so
    the kernel can quiesce it — stop new entries, drain current ones —
    without a search for hidden callers.
    The restart protocol: a fault attributed to a cell (wild access into its
    arena, invariant trip, watchdog-detected hang inside it) quiesces the
    cell, revokes the ledger, reinitializes from scratch, and replays a
    small intent journal. Crash-only design, applied inside the kernel: a
    cell's recovery path IS its boot path, so recovery is tested every boot.
    First cell: the block/SD driver — it has real hardware faults (yank the
    card mid-I/O), a natural quiesce point (the request queue), and a
    bounded ledger. The USB and network stacks (order.txt Phase 7) are built
    AS cells from birth, never retrofitted.

V3. THE INVARIANT ENGINE AND TIME TRAVEL
    lockdep generalized: subsystems DECLARE invariants ("no page is both in
    the free list and the page cache", "an IRQ is masked only if its owner
    cell is quiesced", "a task is on at most one wait queue") and the engine
    checks them continuously and cheaply — on state transitions, not by
    scanning. An invariant trip is attributed to a cell and triggers the V2
    restart path instead of a panic. Declared invariants also become the
    fault-attribution oracle: they are how the kernel decides WHICH cell is
    at fault.
    kdb grows from a live debugger into a time-travel console: replay the
    flight recorder backwards from any fault, filtered by cell, lock, task,
    or CPU. The panic path becomes a triage path: snapshot, attribute,
    restart, log — halting only when attribution fails or the fault is in
    the resilient core itself.

V4. THE EL2 WARDEN
    A thin (~2-3 KLoC, non-VHE) hypervisor layer under the kernel with two
    jobs. First: Stage-2-protect the flight recorder's backing RAM, so no
    wild EL1 write can destroy the evidence — this is what upgrades "we log
    a lot" into "the log is trustworthy BECAUSE the kernel physically cannot
    corrupt it", the difference between a logging feature and an
    architecture. Second: the ultimate cell restart — snapshot and
    micro-reboot the ENTIRE EL1 kernel while preserving user memory and the
    recorder, for faults that attribution places in the core itself.
    The warden is deliberately the smallest pillar and the last built. It is
    also the first step of order.txt Phase 7's "virtualization via EL2":
    guest OSes later become tenants beside the EL1 kernel under the same
    monitor.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HARDWARE WE LEAN ON  (and hardware we do not have)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The BCM2711's Cortex-A72 is ARMv8.0-A. Be honest about what that means:
NO PAC, NO BTI, NO MTE, NO LSE atomics (lock-free structures are LDXR/STXR
loops), NO VHE (the warden is the classic split non-VHE EL2 design). The
vision is built on what the chip DOES have:

  - Watchdog + reset-surviving PM bits: reboot-reason codes and a crash
    cursor that survive even a hard hang, so the next boot always knows why.
  - RAM persistence across warm reset: the recorder's reserved region is
    intact after a watchdog reboot — pre-crash history, post-crash.
  - Per-core generic timers: the recorder's single 54MHz timebase, and the
    supervisor core's independent tick.
  - A spare core as SUPERVISOR: one core runs the health monitor and
    invariant sweeps with its own timer, pings peers via SGIs, and is the
    component entitled to declare another core hung. The monitor must not
    depend on the schedulability of the thing it monitors.
  - PMUv3: always-on sampled profiling cheap enough for release builds,
    feeding the same recorder.
  - GIC-400 affinity/priorities: route a quiesced cell's IRQs away; keep the
    supervisor core's tick unblockable by a wedged peer.
  - Stage-2 translation at EL2: the warden's enforcement mechanism.
  - VideoCore mailbox: temperature, voltage, and throttling events are
    telemetry too — same recorder, same timebase.
  - The framebuffer console: a live health dashboard rendered by the
    supervisor core; the magnifying-glass logo earns its keep.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DESIGN RULES THE VISION IMPOSES TODAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These apply from now on, including during Phases 1-5, so the codebase grows
toward the vision instead of accumulating more retrofit debt:

  1. New subsystems declare their invariants in a comment block at the top of
     the file from day one, even before the engine exists to check them.
     When V3 lands, those comments become declarations.
  2. New drivers are written cell-shaped: init and teardown as clean inverses,
     resources acquired through (eventually: recorded in) one place, entry
     points enumerable. The USB stack is the first mandatory application.
  3. Error paths unwind; PANIC is reserved for boot and for corruption of the
     resilient core itself. (Phase 2 item 4 is this rule, applied backwards.)
  4. Anything that reports state gets a procfs face. Anything that logs, logs
     through the one ring once it exists — no private ring buffers.
  5. On-disk and on-wire data is untrusted input; validation failures are
     attributable events, not silent clamps. (The audit's FAT32 findings are
     this rule, applied backwards.)
  6. Every recovery path gets a fault-injection test that exercises it. A
     recovery path that has never run is a second bug waiting behind the
     first.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MEASURES OF SUCCESS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The vision is falsifiable. We hold ourselves to numbers:

  - RECORDER OVERHEAD: under 2% CPU with every producer enabled, measured by
    the benchmark suite, enforced in release builds. If it costs more, fix
    the record format.
  - NO UNEXPLAINED FAILURE: any hang, panic, or corruption reproducible in
    the test suite must be diagnosable from the recorder alone — no printf
    archaeology, no re-running with extra logging. Each one that is not is a
    recorder gap, and gets an event added.
  - RECOVERY RATE: the fault-injection campaign (bit flips in cell arenas,
    failed allocations, wedged hardware, corrupt on-disk data) reports the
    fraction of faults each cell survives. The number is published in
    docs/, per release, whatever it is.
  - HONEST ATTRIBUTION: misattributed faults (blamed on the wrong cell) are
    counted and treated as first-class bugs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW THE ROADMAP SERVES THIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Phases 1-5 (order.txt): unchanged in substance — the foundation IS the
    prerequisite. Items that seed a pillar are tagged (V1 seed) etc. inline:
    the dmesg ring, the watchdog, strace-lite, per-CPU exception stacks, the
    refcounted-object rule all grow into vision machinery.
  Phase 6 (order.txt): the vision made concrete — recorder, cells, invariant
    engine, time travel, warden, in dependency order, each with a "Done"
    that names a demonstrable capability.
  Phase 7 (order.txt): the old strategic extensions, re-read through the
    vision: USB/networking arrive as cells, EL2 virtualization grows out of
    the warden, and the health dashboard rides the graphics work.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ROADS NOT TAKEN  (recorded so we do not re-litigate them)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three other directions were seriously considered and set aside, not rejected:

  - Capability-hardened hybrid (per-process handle tables, EL2 as a
    capability nexus): deepest research value, but rewires the syscall ABI
    we are actively building, and pays off only at the end.
  - Time-triggered partition kernel (static schedules, core isolation, cache
    coloring; hard real time on COTS hardware): best differentiation per
    effort, but freezes the kernel's concurrency structure before Phase 5
    finishes evolving it.
  - Single-address-space Wasm kernel (SASOS, WASI personality, guard-region
    linear memories): most radical and most publishable, but a second
    kernel personality living inside the first.

What we keep from them: the thin EL2 monitor is the SAME component in all
four directions wearing different hats (capability mirror / partition freezer
/ warden). Building the warden (V4) keeps every one of those doors open
without committing to any. If the vision ever shifts, V4 is the pivot point,
and nothing in V1-V3 is wasted — a capability kernel or an RTOS wants a
flight recorder too.

================================================================================
The test for any proposal, from either file, stays the same: does it make the
kernel easier to see through, or harder? Perspicua chooses the former.
